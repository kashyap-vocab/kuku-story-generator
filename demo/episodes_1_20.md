# Episodes 1-20

Episode 1: The Last Mile

The alarm on my phone is a jagged mess of noise. I hit the snooze button. My hand shakes a little. It’s that twitch from the crash. I don't think about it. I just want the money.

I sit on the edge of my mattress. The room smells like old grease and damp carpet. I pull on my jacket. It’s heavy. I grab my helmet and check the bike. The chain is tight. The tires are full. 

I open the app. My phone screen is cracked in the corner. 

A notification pops up. New shift. High priority. 

I scroll through the details. The destination is 'The Oasis.' It’s a massive apartment complex on the edge of the industrial zone. I look at the delivery list. My eyes narrow. 

One shift. Forty individual units. 

"You gotta be kidding me," I mutter. 

Forty different apartments in one building? That’s not a route. That’s a marathon. I look at the pay. It’s huge. More than a week’s worth of standard gigs. 

I think about my mom's hospital bills. I think about the debt collectors. I tap the screen. 

Accepted.

I kick the bike to life. The engine hums in my chest. I swing the thermal bag over my shoulder. It’s heavy. It feels like it’s filled with lead instead of food.

I pull out onto the street. The city is gray. Streetlights flicker. I follow the GPS. 

The route starts to make sense. Or it doesn't. It’s not a smooth flow. The app tells me to go to the basement, then up to the tenth floor, then back down to the second. It’s a zigzag. It’s a mess.

"Hey, Dispatch?" I say. I press the button on my handlebars. "You seeing this route? It's forty stops in one building. Is the app glitched?"

Silence. Just the hum of the wind.

"Dispatch? You there?"

Nothing. Just static. 

I keep riding. The Oasis looms ahead. It’s a brutalist concrete block. It looks like a tomb for people who wanted to be modern. It’s well-kept. Too well-kept. The lights in the windows are dim. Most of them are dark.

I pull the bike into the front driveway. The tires crunch on gravel. I kill the engine. The silence is sudden. It hits me like a physical weight. 

I look at the phone. The first address is Apartment 101. I’m standing in front of the lobby. The glass doors are tall and dark.

I reach for the 'Start Delivery' button. My thumb hovers over the glass.

*Ping.*

The phone vibrates in my hand. The screen flashes a bright, neon green. 

*Delivery Complete.*

I freeze. I don't move. I haven't even put my foot on the ground. I haven't touched the door.

I stare at the screen. The text is clear. *Delivery 1 of 40: Complete.*

I look at the next address. It’s Apartment 104. Same building. Same lobby. 

I don't move. I just look at the phone.

Episode 2: The Last Mile

I stand in the lobby. The glass doors are behind me. The air inside feels heavy. It's like the atmosphere changed the second I stepped over the threshold. My ears pop. It’s a sharp, stinging pressure in my sinuses. 

I pull out my phone. I press the button for Dispatch.

Static. Just a harsh, white noise. 

"Dispatch? You there?" I say.

Nothing but the hiss of the radio.

I look at the app. It's still showing 40 deliveries left. I need to get moving. I need the money. I walk toward the elevator bank. The floor feels like it's vibrating under my boots. Every step feels like I'm wading through waist-deep water.

I get into the elevator. It’s a metal cage. The light overhead flickers with a rhythmic *click-clack*. I press the button for 4. 

The lift starts to move. A high-pitched hum fills the space. It’s not the motor. It’s coming from inside the walls. It’s a steady, drilling sound. My ears ache. I feel like I'm underwater. I can barely hear my own breathing.

The doors groan open on the fourth floor. 

The hallway is dim. The carpet is a gross, mottled gray. It smells like old dust. Underneath that, there’s something else. Something sharp. It smells like burnt hair. Or maybe scorched plastic.

I check the app. Apartment 402. 

I see the door. It’s slightly ajar. A sliver of darkness waits behind it. I hesitate. My hand shakes on the handle. I push it open.

The room is empty. No couch. No TV. Just bare, peeling walls and a layer of gray dust on the floor. The smell of burnt hair is way stronger in here. It makes my throat tighten.

I take one step inside.

*Ping.*

I jump. My phone vibrates in my pocket. I pull it out.

*Delivery 2 of 40: Complete.*

I stare at the screen. I haven't touched a single thing. I haven't even put a package down. I just stood in the doorway.

I back out of the room. My chest feels tight. I turn to head back toward the elevator, but I stop.

Someone is there.

At the far end of the hallway, a figure stands by a wall. She's wearing a white dress. The fabric looks frayed. Her body doesn't look solid. The edges of her arms and shoulders blur into the air like smoke. She’s facing the wall. She isn't moving.

I blink. 

She’s there. A pale shape in the gloom.

I blink again.

The hallway is empty. Just the dim light and the smell of smoke.

I stand in the center of the hall. My breath comes in short, ragged gasps. I look back at the open door of 402. 

*Ping.*

The phone vibrates again. 

*Delivery 3 of 40: Complete.*

I haven't moved. I’m still standing in the middle of the hall. I look down at my left hand. It's shaking. The tremor is back, and it’s getting worse.

Episode 3: The Last Mile

I stand in the middle of the hallway, my chest heaving. The pressure in my ears is a physical weight. It feels like my head is in a pressurized tank. I look at the phone. 

The app is still acting up. I tap the "Call Dispatch" button again. 

Static. Just that same white noise. It’s constant.

I turn toward the stairs. I just want to get out of this hallway. I need to find a security desk or a manager. I need to tell someone the app is broken. 

I take three steps. 

The hallway stretches. It feels longer. I look back at the door I just left. It looks like it's a hundred yards away now. The drilling hum in the walls gets louder. It's a steady, vibrating thrum that makes my teeth ache. 

I stop. I have to find a way to fix this. My head is spinning.

Suddenly, my phone chirps. 

It's not the app. It's a real call. 

I answer it. 

"Maintain the flow," a woman says. Her voice is flat. It's cold. Like she's reading a manual. 

"Who is this?" I ask. 

"Ignore the glitches," she says. "Just keep moving."

"The app is lagging," I say. "It’s marking things as complete on its own. I’m seeing... stuff. People."

"It is not lagging," she says. "Continue the route."

"What glitches?" 

"Maintain the flow."

Then the line breaks. A high-pitched, metallic screech tears through the speaker. It's so sharp I have to pull the phone away from my ear. My ears ring. Then, silence.

I look back at the app. 

403. 404. 405. 

The hallway is dim. The smell of burnt hair is heavy here. It’s like a wet, hot ash. I look at the shadows in the corners. They don't move. The lights flicker, but the shadows stay pinned to the floor. 

A sharp jab hits my temple. I stumble. 

I reach door 403. 

It’s just like 402. Bare walls. Dust. Peeling paint. I stand there and wait. I wait for the *ping*. 

Five seconds pass. Ten. 

Nothing. 

I look at the app. The checkmark for 403 is gray. It's locked. 

I look at my phone. The name 'Sarah' is gone. It's just a string of numbers now. 

*Whisper.*

A girl's voice comes from behind me. It's soft. Like she's underwater. 

I spin around. 

Nothing. Just the smell of smoke. 

I turn back to 403. I reach out. My fingers graze the wood of the door frame. 

The drilling hum stops instantly. 

The silence is heavy. It's a vacuum. I can hear my own pulse in my neck. 

*Thud.*

A heavy sound comes from inside 403. Like a chair hitting the floor. 

The app screen turns deep red. It pulses like a heartbeat. 

A message appears in the chat: *DO NOT OPEN.*

The door handle begins to turn. The door creaks inward.

Episode 4: The Last Mile

The door swings inward on a rusted hinge. It hits the wall with a dull thud. I step inside and the air hits me like a physical blow. It tastes like copper and old electricity. It’s heavy. It’s dry. Everything in the room is covered in a thick layer of grey dust. A sofa, a coffee table, a lamp. All of it is buried under a shroud of grey. 

In the center of the room, a single chair faces a blank wall. 

The drilling hum from the walls is different here. It’s deafening. It vibrates in my ribs. It makes my teeth ache. 

I see him.

A man is sitting in the chair. He isn't solid. He looks like grey smoke swirling in water. He’s translucent. His body is a blur of shifting particles. He doesn't turn around. He just sits there, perfectly still. 

I take a step forward. My boot crunches on the dust. The sound is too loud in the silence. 

I look down at my phone. The screen is still glowing red. The words *DO NOT OPEN* are flashing. 

I look back at the smoke-man. 

His head tilts. It’s a slow, jerky movement. A sound comes from his throat. It isn't a human voice. It’s a radio station caught between frequencies. It’s a wet, static-filled rasp. 

A sharp pain stabs my temple. It’s the same spot where the pressure usually sits. My left hand starts to shake. It’s not just a tremor anymore. My fingers are twitching uncontrollably. 

The smoke-man turns his head fully. His face is a mess of features, like a photograph left out in the rain. He points a long, trembling finger at the black box in my hands. 

The box is humming now. It’s a low-frequency vibration. It makes my vision blur. 

The man's mouth moves. His voice cuts through the hum. It sounds like it's coming from the bottom of a deep well.

"It's getting louder," he says.

The words don't just enter my ears. They vibrate in my skull. 

I recoil. My heel catches on a loose floorboard. I stumble back, nearly dropping the box. The man's eyes—if they are eyes—fix on the box. The intensity of his gaze makes my skin crawl. 

The hum from the box spikes. It turns into a piercing whine. It’s high-pitched. It hurts to listen to.

I scramble backward. My heart hammers against my ribs. I want to drop the box and run for the door, but my fingers won't let go. The weight of the box feels like it tripled. It’s heavy. It’s pulling my arm down. 

I look at the app. The red screen has changed. The warning is gone. A new message is scrolling across the glass.

*TRANSFER IN PROGRESS.*

Behind me, the smoke-man begins to flicker. His form becomes jagged. He’s moving faster now, his movements frantic. He’s losing his shape.

Then, the smoke clears for a split second. His face becomes sharp. Clear. He looks at me, and his mouth opens wide.

"Elias... stop it..."

I stumble out of 405 and into the hallway. My breath comes in short, jagged gasps. I run toward the stairs. I hit the landing, but there are no stairs. 

There’s just another hallway.

I turn left. It’s the same hallway. I turn right. It’s the same hallway. I look back at 405. It’s still there, but it looks like it’s at the end of a long, receding tunnel. 

I start to jog. My boots thud heavily on the carpet. I pass a 'Maintenance' sign. I keep running and pass the same 'Maintenance' sign again. And again. Every time I get close to the elevator, the corridor bends. It loops me back toward the center of the floor.

I pull out my phone to check the map. The screen shows a single dot. It’s my icon. It’s spinning in a circle in a gray void.

I stop. I’m sweating. The salt stings my eyes. I look at my watch. 3:00 AM. I look at my phone. 3:00 AM.

I watch the seconds tick. One. Two. Three. 

They don't move. The hands on the watch stay still. The numbers on the phone don't change.

I shake the phone. I scream at the screen. Nothing happens. 

I look down at my hands. They look pale. The skin at my fingertips is starting to look thin, almost translucent at the edges. 

A faint, wet rasp sounds behind me. 

"Elias... stop it..."

The voice isn't coming from the hallway. It’s coming from the box. 

I look at the phone again. The time is still 3:00 AM. The 'Next Delivery' notification has changed.

*Next Delivery: Apartment 405.*

I look at the door I just left. It’s the only way out, but the app says I have to go back inside.

I reach for the photo. My hand trembles. As my fingers touch the frame, the hum in the walls becomes a deafening roar.
