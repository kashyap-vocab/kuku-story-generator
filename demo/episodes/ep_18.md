Episode 18: The Last Mile

The app says 4:05 AM.

I’m on my knees. My head feels like it’s being split open by a dull blade. Sarah’s voice—that wet, screaming thing—is still ringing in my ears. But now, the hum in the room has changed. It’s not just a drone anymore. It’s a thrum. It has a rhythm. Thump-thump. Thump-thump. It feels like a heartbeat coming from the floorboards.

I try to stand up. My legs feel like they're made of lead. I'm struggling to stay upright. I look at the wall. A door is there. It wasn't there a minute ago. It’s a heavy metal slab with 'Maintenance - Authorized Personnel Only' stenciled in fading white paint.

I crawl toward it. My hands are flickering. I can see the grey bricks of the wall through my palms. I’m losing my density. I reach out and push. The door groans open.

I stumble into a corridor. It’s narrow. It’s tight. The ceiling is low. The walls are covered in exposed copper veins. They aren't just wires. They pulse with a sickly yellow light. The air tastes like wet earth and old metal. It’s heavy in my lungs.

Footsteps hit the floor ahead of me. They're heavy. They're uneven.

A man rounds the corner. He’s old. He’s wearing a tattered bathrobe. He’s clutching a heavy flashlight like a weapon. He stops when he sees me. His eyes are wide. He looks like he hasn't slept in years.

He points a shaking finger at the walls.

"Don't look at them," the old man says. His voice is a raspy whisper. "Don't look at the walls."

I look anyway. I see shadows moving behind the copper veins. They look like hands. They look like faces pressed against the inside of the metal.

The man grabs my arm. His grip is like a vice. It's surprisingly strong for someone so thin.

"You gotta keep moving," he whispers into my ear. "They're feeding on the memories of the dead. They're hungry. And yours are next."

I try to speak. My throat feels full of sand. I can't make a sound.

A cold shiver hits my spine. I try to pull away from his grip, but he holds me tight.

"I can't let you go," he says. His voice is trembling. "If you stop, they'll take everything."

The hum in the corridor spikes. It hits me in the chest like a physical wall. I can feel my ribs ache. The lights overhead flicker. They die.

In the strobe light, the old man’s face starts to warp. His features are melting into the shadows. He opens his mouth and screams. It isn't a human sound. It’s a digital screech. It’s a layered roar of a hundred different voices.

I watch in horror. His skin turns into grey, flickering static. His body stretches. He blurs. He becomes a jagged hole in the air. Then, he's gone.

The screaming stops. The silence is worse.

I’m alone in the dark. I can still see the yellow glow of the copper veins. I look down at my hand. My wrist is almost gone. I can see the floor through my skin. I try to think of my mother. I try to remember her name. 

Nothing. 

I try to remember my own last name. 

Nothing. Just a grey void where my life used to be.

The hum starts again. This time, it’s a whisper. It’s coming from the walls.

"Subject 41, proceed to the next waypoint."

I look at my phone. The screen is cracked. A new delivery address appears. It’s not a room number. It's a set of GPS coordinates. They point to the center of the building's foundation.
