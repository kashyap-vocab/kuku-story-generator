Episode 1: The Last Mile

The alarm on my phone is a jagged mess of noise. I hit the snooze button. My hand shakes a little. It’s that twitch from the crash. I don't think about it. I just want the money.

I sit on the edge of my mattress. The room smells like old grease and damp carpet. I pull on my jacket. It’s heavy. I grab my helmet and check the bike. The chain is tight. The tires are full. 

I open the app. My phone screen is cracked in the corner. 

A notification pops up. New shift. High priority. 

I scroll through the details. The destination is 'The Oasis.' It’s a massive apartment complex on the edge of the industrial zone. I look at the delivery list. My eyes narrow. 

One shift. Forty individual units. 

"You gotta be kidding me," I mutter. 

Forty different apartments in one building? That’s not a route. That’s a marathon. I look at the pay. It’s huge. More than a week’s worth of standard gigs. 

I think about my mom's hospital bills. I think about the debt collectors. I tap the screen. 

Accepted.

I kick the bike to life. The engine hums in my chest. I swing the thermal bag over my shoulder. It’s heavy. It feels like it’s filled with lead instead of food.

I pull out onto the street. The city is gray. Streetlights flicker. I follow the GPS. 

The route starts to make sense. Or it doesn't. It’s not a smooth flow. The app tells me to go to the basement, then up to the tenth floor, then back down to the second. It’s a zigzag. It’s a mess.

"Hey, Dispatch?" I say. I press the button on my handlebars. "You seeing this route? It's forty stops in one building. Is the app glitched?"

Silence. Just the hum of the wind.

"Dispatch? You there?"

Nothing. Just static. 

I keep riding. The Oasis looms ahead. It’s a brutalist concrete block. It looks like a tomb for people who wanted to be modern. It’s well-kept. Too well-kept. The lights in the windows are dim. Most of them are dark.

I pull the bike into the front driveway. The tires crunch on gravel. I kill the engine. The silence is sudden. It hits me like a physical weight. 

I look at the phone. The first address is Apartment 101. I’m standing in front of the lobby. The glass doors are tall and dark.

I reach for the 'Start Delivery' button. My thumb hovers over the glass.

*Ping.*

The phone vibrates in my hand. The screen flashes a bright, neon green. 

*Delivery Complete.*

I freeze. I don't move. I haven't even put my foot on the ground. I haven't touched the door.

I stare at the screen. The text is clear. *Delivery 1 of 40: Complete.*

I look at the next address. It’s Apartment 104. Same building. Same lobby. 

I don't move. I just look at the phone.
