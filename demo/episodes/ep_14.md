Episode 14: The Last Mile

The app says 3:24 AM.

I stand in the center of the basement. The hum is a physical weight on my lungs. It presses against my ribs until it hurts to inhale. I look at the shadows. They aren't just ghosts. They are visual artifacts. They look like people made of broken glass and static. They vibrate with a high-frequency buzz that makes my teeth ache.

A group of them huddles in a corner near the vats of black liquid. They move in a frantic, jerky loop. One man is the worst. His face is a mess of jagged pixels. He's trying to turn a door handle that isn't there. His arm swings over and over. His body flickers like a dying light bulb.

I try to back away. My legs feel heavy. It’s like walking through waist-deep water. I look at my palm. The grey drywall of the basement is clearly visible through the skin of my hand. I can see the studs in the wall. The air tastes like copper and burnt hair.

I realize it. They aren't just haunting the place. They are trapped in a loop of their final moments. The building is replaying their deaths. I feel my own body losing its weight. I'm becoming less solid with every second.

The man with the door handle stops. His head tilts at an impossible angle. A crackling sound like radio static comes from his throat. He turns toward me. His face isn't a face anymore. It’s a swirling vortex of grey noise and white light.

The hum spikes. It hits me like a physical blow to my chest. A hot, thick liquid trickles from my left nostril. I watch a drop fall. It vanishes into the air an inch before it hits the floor. The man's mouth opens wide. A sound like a scream made of grinding metal fills the room. My vision starts to strobe. The drywall behind my hand seems to be pulling me inward.

I fall to my knees. The floor feels soft, like I'm falling into a bed of ash. I can't see my feet. They’re just a blur of grey. The man in the corner screams again. The frequency makes my ears bleed. The black liquid in the vats begins to ripple. It moves in sync with his screams.

A new message flashes on my phone. It repeats over and over. 'EXTRACTION IN PROGRESS. 99%.'

I try to stand. My arm feels like it's made of smoke. I look at the man again. He’s closer now. He isn't walking. He’s just appearing closer with every flicker of the light. He reaches out a hand of jagged glass. The air around his fingers warps. It bends the light like a heat haze.

My phone rings. It’s Sarah. I can barely hear her over the grinding scream. I hold the phone with a hand that is now almost entirely transparent.

"Get out," she says. Her voice is a tiny thread of sound. "The data is peaking. You're the bridge. If you stay, you'll be consumed."

I try to speak. My throat is dry. I look at the man. His hand is inches from my face. The static from his body starts to coat my skin like frost.

I feel a memory slip away. The smell of my mother's perfume. It's gone. A blank space in my head.

The man's fingers touch my forehead. The world turns white.

I feel a sensation of being pulled through a straw. My bones feel like they're turning into liquid. The hum is no longer a sound. It's my entire reality. I see the vats of black liquid rising up like towers. I see the server rack expanding. Its cables become veins that reach for me.

I try to scream. No sound comes out. I'm not in the basement anymore. I'm inside the hum. I'm inside the data.

I feel my name slipping away. I can't remember my name. I can't remember my home. I can't remember anything but the hum.

The man's hand touches my forehead, and for a second, I don't see the basement. I see a different room. A bedroom. A girl's face.

And then, the hum swallows everything.
