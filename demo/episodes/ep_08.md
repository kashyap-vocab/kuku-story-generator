Episode 8: The Last Mile

The app says 3:05 AM.

The hallway is pitch black. My phone screen is the only light. It’s a white glare against my face. The air tastes like copper. It’s heavy. My heart hits my ribs like a hammer. I look at the screen. The red letters are still there. *RE-ROUTE: APARTMENT 412.*

I take a step. My boots make no sound on the carpet. The sobbing stops. The silence is worse than the noise. I move toward the door marked 412. The hum in the walls changes. It’s not a whine anymore. It’s a low, rhythmic thrum. It feels like a heart beating behind the drywall. I reach out. My hand shakes. I touch the doorknob. It’s ice cold. I turn it.

The door creaks open.

The inside of the apartment is bathed in a pale blue light. It smells like old water. It smells like rotting fruit. I step inside.

The room is a mess. Furniture is overturned. Dust covers everything like a shroud. In the corner, a girl is sitting on the floor. She looks about twelve. She wears a faded dress. Her skin is the color of wet ash. She isn't solid. When she moves, she looks like a glitch in a video. Her hair falls over her face.

"Elias..." she whispers.

The voice sounds like it's coming from the bottom of a deep well.

"Elias, help me."

She turns her head. Her eyes are milky white. She looks like she's underwater.

"I can't find it," she says. "I can't find my phone. I need to call them."

She reaches out. Her fingers are translucent. She touches my forearm. A jolt of cold electricity shoots through me. My skin goes numb instantly. I feel a sharp, stabbing pain behind my eyes. For a second, I see a flash of a sunny backyard. Then it's gone. I try to think of the grass. I can't remember what color it was.

I stumble back. My breath comes in ragged gasps. "Who are you?" I ask. My voice sounds thin.

The girl doesn't hear me. She keeps crying. No tears come down her face. The grey smoke of her body drifts toward me.

"Help me," she repeats.

She points to a pile of electronics in the corner. They look melted together. A laptop, a tablet, and a bunch of wires are fused into a jagged lump of plastic. It looks like it was hit by a high-voltage surge.

I feel a heavy weight in my pocket. I reach in and pull out a small, metallic component from my delivery bag. It looks like a specialized sensor. I hold it out.

Chloe’s head snaps toward it. Her jaw drops. "Is that... is that it?" she gasps.

The air in the room begins to vibrate. The hum becomes a physical force. My vision blurs into streaks of blue and grey. I move toward her. My legs feel heavy.

"Here," I say. My voice is barely a whisper.

The blue light intensifies. Chloe reaches for the component. Her fingers pass through my hand, but the sensation is like dipping my arm into dry ice. She grabs it.

The moment she touches it, her entire body shudders. A cloud of grey static erupts from her. It’s loud. It’s a crackle of electricity and a scream that sounds like tearing metal.

Then, she’s gone.

The blue light vanishes. The room goes dark. It smells like ozone and scorched hair. I stand alone in the center of the room. I try to take a step back, but my feet won't move. I look down at my hands. They are becoming more transparent. I can see the floorboards through my palms.

A new ping sounds on my phone. It’s loud. It pierces the silence. I look at the screen.

*Final Package - Apartment 444.*

I try to move, but my body feels like it's made of lead. I look at my reflection in a cracked mirror on the wall. My face is blurring. The features are smoothing out. It’s like a photo being rubbed by a thumb.

I feel a sudden, sharp pull in my mind. I try to remember my middle name. I search for it. There's just a void. It's like the word was never there. A cold sweat breaks out on my forehead.

I look back at the phone. The 'Final Package' prompt is pulsing. The app isn't just giving me a route. It's dragging me. I feel my body start to tilt. The floor is becoming a slide. I'm being pulled toward the door of 412, but the hallway outside seems to be stretching. It's lengthening into an impossible distance.

I look at the keys in my hand. They are glowing with a faint, sickly light. I try to scream, but as my mouth opens, I realize I can't remember the word for 'help'.
