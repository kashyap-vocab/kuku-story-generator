Episode 19: The Last Mile

The app says 4:12 AM.

I move toward the coordinates. My feet drag on the grit of the floor. The hum is a physical weight on my shoulders. It presses down on my chest. It makes my ribs ache with every step. 

I find a door. It's a maintenance crawlspace. I squeeze inside. The space is tiny. My shoulders rub against the rough stone. The walls here hum so loud my ears ring. It’s a constant, vibrating roar. It feels like my teeth are rattling in my head.

I see a rusted metal desk in the corner. A set of blueprints sits on top of it. I reach out. My hand flickers. I can see the grey stone through my palm. I grab the paper anyway.

The blueprints show the floor plan of the Oasis. There are forty red dots across the map. They aren't just random marks. They form a perfect geometric pattern. It’s a grid. I look at the way the hallways intersect. I see how the copper veins in the walls run between the dots. 

The building isn't just a place to live. It’s a giant antenna. The architecture is designed to trap and amplify sound waves. It's a frequency trap. The dots are the focal points. 

I look at the dots again. They align perfectly with the apartments where I delivered the packages. Every single one. The packages aren't just deliveries. They are triggers. They start the vibration. 

I try to think of my mother. I want to see her face. I want to remember the color of her hair. I see nothing. All I see is the yellow pulse of the copper veins. The memory is gone. It’s just a grey void. 

I realize what I am. I’m not just a delivery rider. I’m a biological relay. My brain, the injury, my body. I’m stabilizing the frequency. I’m the bridge they’re using to pull the dead out of the walls and into the system.

My hand flickers again. It’s getting harder to hold my own weight. I’m becoming less solid with every second I spend in this room.

My pocket vibrates. The phone is screaming against my hip. I pull it out. The screen is a spiderweb of cracks. 

I answer it. 

I don't hear Sarah. I don't hear one person. I hear forty people. It’s a layered chorus. They are all screaming at once. The voices are different pitches. They are perfectly synchronized with the thrum of the walls. It's a wall of sound. It's a nightmare.

The phone rings again, and Sarah's voice is now a layered chorus of forty different people screaming at once.
