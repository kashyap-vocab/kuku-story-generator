Episode 10: The Last Mile

The app says 3:12 AM.

I stand over the man in the uniform. He’s a heap of blue fabric and dead weight on the floor. My teeth ache from the hum. It’s a constant, vibrating pressure against my skull. I look at my hands. They’re barely there. I can see the grey pattern of the carpet through my palms. I'm becoming a ghost while I'm still breathing.

The building pulls me. It's a physical tug in my chest. My boots drag across the floor as I move toward the end of the hall. The air changes. It gets sharp. It smells like ozone and burnt hair. 

I reach Apartment 444. The door creaks. It doesn't need a hand to turn the knob. It just swings wide.

Inside, the dust hangs in the air like a fog. Old furniture sits under white sheets. In the corner, I see her.

It’s Chloe.

She looks more solid than she did in the other room. Her face flickers like a bad TV signal. She’s sitting on the floor. She looks at me and reaches out.

The package in my bag starts to vibrate. It’s a small metallic piece. It glows with a faint blue light.

A sharp pain spikes in my head. It feels like a hot needle behind my eyes. I try to think of my first bike. I can see the frame in my mind. It's red. The handlebars are... 

The color is gone. The memory of the red paint is just a hole in my head. It's just... gone.

I feel a cold spot open up in my chest.

I look at Chloe. She’s weeping. No sound comes out of her mouth.

"I'm so cold," she says. Her voice sounds like a radio between stations. Static and hiss.

I hold out the metallic component. It's the trigger. The app wanted this. 

Chloe’s hand trembles. She reaches out. Her fingers touch the metal.

A jolt of electricity snaps through my arm. It burns. I see her body start to break apart. She turns into white static. It sprays into the air like sparks.

"Thank you," she whispers.

Then she’s gone. The room goes silent. The blue light vanishes. The package is just... gone. It's like the air swallowed it.

I feel a massive drain in my brain. It's like a vacuum is sucking out my thoughts. I try to think of my street. The one where I grew up. I try to picture the house number.

Nothing. It's a blank space. I don't know the name of my street.

I turn to leave. The door slams shut before I can reach it.

The hum spikes again. My vision strobes. White. Grey. White. Grey.

I look at my phone. The screen is cracked. The app is still running. I look down at my knuckles. I can see the floor clearly through my skin now. I'm almost transparent.

I try to scream. My throat is dry. Only a rasp comes out.

The phone pings.

*Final Package - Apartment 444*
