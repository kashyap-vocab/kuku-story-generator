Episode 13: The Last Mile

The app says 3:20 AM.

My head is a drum. Someone is hitting it with a lead pipe. The strobe effect from the booster still makes my eyes water. My left hand feels like a heavy block of stone. I try to pull it back, but my fingers just drag against the metal casing.

The phone in my pocket starts to buzz. It’s a heavy, jagged vibration. I pull it out. An unknown number is calling.

I answer. My voice sounds like it’s coming from a long way off.

"Who is this?" I ask.

"You're off-track," a woman says. Her voice is flat. It’s cold. Like she’s reading a list of groceries. "Move to the basement maintenance hub immediately. You have specialized hardware to deliver."

I look at the copper veins in the wall. They look like they're pulsing. "Why the basement?" I ask.

"Keep moving," she says. She doesn't answer me.

I start to walk. The hallway is a mess. It’s too narrow. Then it’s too wide. The walls feel like they're moving, shifting under my touch. I look at my hand. It’s worse. I can see the grey drywall through my palm now. My skin is getting thinner.

The smell of ozone is everywhere. It burns the back of my throat. A sharp needle of pain shoots into my temple. I try to think about my house. I can't. I can't remember what I ate today. I try to remember my front door. It's just a blank space in my head. A hole.

I keep walking. The app keeps updating my position. It’s a glowing green line leading me down.

I reach the basement. It’s massive. It’s a warehouse of industrial junk. Huge vats of that black liquid sit in the corners. They look like oil slicks. The hum here is a physical weight. It pushes against my chest. It makes it hard to take a full breath.

I see a heavy metal crate near a server rack that’s the size of a room. I walk over and pry it open. It’s full of copper coils and transmitters. They look high-grade. I look at the app.

'Delivery Complete.'

The phone crackles. Sarah is back. Her voice is lower now. It’s deeper.

"Stay put," she says. "Do not look at the visual artifacts in the hallway. They aren't real. They are just data noise."

I look past the crate.

The shadows are full of them. They aren't like Chloe. They aren't smoke. They look like people made of broken glass and static. They are frozen in mid-scream. They're flickering like bad TV signals. 

One woman is reaching out toward me. Her face looks like it's underwater. The air temperature drops. The hum reaches a screaming pitch. It feels like my vision is tearing apart.

Sarah’s voice comes through the phone again. It’s distorted. It’s overlapping with the static screams of the people in the shadows.

"Don't look at them," she whispers.

I can't stop looking. I see their eyes. They're full of white noise.

The phone in my hand turns bright white. A new message appears on the screen:

'DATA INTEGRITY AT 98%. BEGIN FINAL EXTRACTION.'
