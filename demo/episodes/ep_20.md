Episode 20: The Last Mile

The app says 4:15 AM.

The screaming doesn't stop when I put the phone down. It’s not coming from the speaker anymore. It’s coming from my teeth. It’s coming from the back of my eyes. I press my palms against my ears, but the sound is inside my skull. It’s the layered chorus of forty people, all crying out in different tones. 

I look at my hands. They’re shaking. No, they’re flickering. Like a bad lightbulb. I can see the grey stone of the floor through the skin of my palms. I’m becoming see-through. The phone in my grip is hot. It’s vibrating with a steady, heavy pulse. It matches the thrum of the walls. I try to hit the power button. It won't move. My thumb just slides over the plastic. 

The voices aren't just in the phone. They're being pulled through me. I feel a sharp, stinging heat behind my eyes. It feels like my brain is being cooked from the inside.

The hum hits a new peak. It’s a wall of pressure. I stumble forward. My body feels light. I feel less dense, like I’m made of smoke. I push against a heavy, rusted door. It wasn't there a second ago. I move through it and the world changes.

It feels like I’m underwater. The air tastes like copper. It’s heavy. In the center of the room, a figure stands. It’s a girl. Chloe. She looks like a ripple in a pond. She’s standing in a pool of grey static. It pools on the floor like spilled milk. Her face is a blur of moving pixels. Her eyes are wide. They look terrified. 

Behind her, the wall is weeping. A thick, yellow gas hisses out of a crack in the stone. It smells like old sulfur and wet hair. 

I try to speak. My throat feels full of sand. I take a step toward her. The floor feels soft. It feels like walking on a sponge. 

Chloe turns to me. Her mouth moves, but no sound comes out. She points at the gas. It’s moving fast. It’s crawling toward me like a living thing. 

A sharp pain cracks through my head. A memory flashes. A birthday cake. Three candles. Then it's gone. I can't remember the color of the frosting. I can't remember who gave it to me. The memory is just a hole in my head. 

The static around Chloe gets louder. She reaches out. Her fingers brush my arm. My whole arm goes numb. The yellow gas hits my face. 

For a second, I see everything. I see the building as a skeleton of light. I see the data flowing like blood through the copper veins in the walls. I see the harvest.

The gas is everywhere now. My lungs burn. I cough until my chest aches. I look at the phone. The screen is glowing white. A text message appears. 

'I'm still here, Elias. Let me in.'

I look up. Chloe is gone. The static has swallowed her whole. The room is silent. Only the hiss of the gas and the heavy thrum of the building remain. 

My legs buckle. I fall to my knees. I can see the floor through my chest. My skin is almost transparent. I look at the phone again. The text is scrolling now.

'Let me in. Let me in. Let me in.'

The door behind me slams shut. It locks with a heavy, mechanical thud. I’m trapped. I look at my hands. They are nearly invisible. I try to feel my pulse. I can't feel my own heartbeat anymore. 

The phone screen turns blood red. A new message appears: 

'OPEN THE DOOR.'
