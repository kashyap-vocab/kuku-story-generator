Episode 17: The Last Mile

The app says 4:02 AM.

The hatch swings wide. A figure in heavy, lead-lined coveralls drops from the opening. They don't look at me. They just move with a heavy, mechanical gait toward a workstation at the back of the room. 

The heat hits me like a physical blow. It’s suffocating. It smells like ozone and burnt hair. The air tastes like copper and old electricity. 

I scramble back against a row of humming servers. My palms hit the drywall. I can see the grey texture of the building through my own skin. I’m becoming a ghost.

On the desk, there's a mess of wires and monitors. A single package sits in the center. It’s one of the triggers. I see my name on the side, but the tape is ripped. 

I move toward it. My legs feel heavy, like they're wading through deep water. I reach out and tear the lid off.

Inside isn't a chip or a sensor. It’s a pulsing, wet lump of grey matter. It looks like a piece of human brain tissue. It shivers against the cardboard.

A sharp spike of pain shoots through my skull. My vision blurs. The room tilts. I can feel a connection, like a wire is being threaded through my spine and into the server rack. The "Harvest" isn't just data. It’s biological. They’re using my body as a bridge to pull the dead out of the walls.

I try to step back, but my knees buckle. The hum in the room jumps an octave. My teeth ache. I feel the vibration in my jawbone. 

I look at my hands again. They're nearly gone. I can see the individual bricks of the wall through my fingers. I try to think of my mom. I want to see her face. I need to remember her eyes.

Nothing. Just a grey void. It’s like trying to grab smoke with my bare hands. The memory is slipping away, dissolving into the hum.

My pocket vibrates. It's violent. It's shaking so hard I almost drop the phone. I pull it out. The screen is a web of cracks, but the call is active.

I swipe to answer. I expect Sarah's cold, professional voice.

Instead, I hear a wet, gurgling sob. It sounds like someone is drowning in thick syrup, trying to scream through a throat full of liquid. It’s a horrible, bubbling sound.

Then, it changes.

It spikes into a high-pitched, ear-piercing screech. It’s so loud I feel my ears bleed. I collapse to my knees, my hands hitting the floor. The sound tears through my head, stripping away another layer of my identity.

The screeching stops instantly. 

The silence is so heavy it feels like my ears are bleeding. Then, a new voice comes through the phone. It’s Sarah. It’s perfectly clear. It’s not coming from the speaker. It’s coming from inside my own skull.

"Don't let go, Elias. We're almost done."
