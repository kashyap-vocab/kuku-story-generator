Episode 7: The Last Mile

The app says 3:01 AM.

The smoke-hand pulls me back. It’s like being dragged by a heavy, wet rope. The keys in my hand burn. The heat is intense. I try to scream, but my throat is full of ash. No sound comes out. 

Then, the floor shakes. The whole room vibrates like a dying engine. The smoke-hand loses its grip. The grey smoke shatters into dust. I fall hard onto the floor. My knees hit the floorboards with a thud.

The hum in the walls changes. It’s a high-pitched whine now. It makes my teeth ache. I look at my hands. They aren't see-through anymore. They’re solid. My skin is pale, almost grey. 

The white box is gone. There’s just a scorched circle on the chair. 

My phone pings. 

*Delivery Completed.*

The screen flickers. A new task appears. I stumble toward the door and push it open.

I’m gasping. My legs feel like jelly. I hit the wall and slide down a few inches. A man is standing there. He’s old. He wears a threadbare cardigan. He’s clutching a framed photo to his chest like a shield. He’s shaking.

I try to speak. My throat is dry.

"You shouldn't be in there," the man says. His voice is thin. He points a trembling finger at the door of 408. "They're still in there."

"Who?" I managed to croak out.

The man looks at the photo. "The building... it remembers," he says. "The gas leak. It didn't just kill them. It trapped them. The walls... they hold the echoes."

"What does that mean?" I ask.

He steps closer. He smells like old paper and menthol. "They're still screaming," he says. "And you... you're the one feeding them."

I look at the keys in my hand. A feeling of nausea hits me. "Why am I feeding them?"

The man’s eyes go wide. He stares at the keys. "Those keys," he whispers. "They belong to the apartment that collapsed. You're carrying the weight of the dead."

I try to drop them. They won't move. They're stuck to my palm like magnets.

The man leans in. His face is inches from mine. "Every time you drop a box, you're giving them a piece of your life," he says. "They're hungry, Elias. They eat memories. They eat time."

He points to my head. "Look at your face. You're fading."

I feel a sharp tug in my brain. I try to think of my home. I try to see my front door. I can't see the color. It's just gone. I look at the man again. His face is starting to blur at the edges.

"Wait," I say.

His voice is a rasp. "The delivery boy... he's the bridge. He's the meal."

He collapses against the wall. He’s still holding the photo. It slides out of his grip. I see a woman and a child. The woman looks exactly like the woman I was trying to remember. My mother.

I reach for the photo. My hand trembles. As my fingers touch the frame, the hum in the walls becomes a deafening roar. 

The lights overhead pop. One by one. The hallway goes dark. 

Only my phone stays on. The screen is a blinding white. Big, red letters appear: *RE-ROUTE: APARTMENT 412.*

The air turns freezing. I can see my breath in the air. From the end of the hall, a new sound starts. It's a wet, sobbing sound.

A girl's voice whispers my name from the darkness.

"Elias... help me."
