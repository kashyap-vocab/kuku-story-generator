Episode 11: The Last Mile

The app says 3:13 AM.

I stand in the center of Apartment 444. The door is closed now. I can see through the wood like it’s made of smoked glass. The air tastes like copper and burnt hair. It’s heavy in my lungs. I look at my hands. They’re almost gone. I see the grey tile of the floor through my knuckles. My skin is a thin, grey veil. 

I try to think of my parents' house. I try to find the kitchen table or the way the light hit the rug. There's nothing. It’s just a hole in my head. A blank, white void where my life used to be.

The hum in the walls spikes. It vibrates in my teeth. It's a physical weight, pushing against my skull. I take a step forward. My boot makes no sound. I feel like I'm walking through deep water.

I look at my phone. The screen is glowing bright white.

*Delivery Complete.*

The text flickers. The screen distorts into a mess of jagged lines. Then, a new message appears.

*Proceed to Apartment 444.*

I blink. I’m already here. I’m standing in the middle of the room. The app says I'm finished, but it's telling me to do it again. It's a loop. My head throbbed with a sharp, rhythmic pulse.

I turn to go back. 

The hallway is gone.

I spin around. There is no door behind me. Just a solid wall of grey drywall. It looks like it was always there. I run to it and bang my fist against the surface. It sounds hollow. 

I turn back to the room. The furniture is moving. The white sheets on the chairs are drifting, floating as if caught in a breeze. There is no wind. The smell of ozone is so strong it makes my eyes water.

I look at the phone again. The screen is a mess of green and white noise. A text box pops up in the center.

*RE-ENTRY REQUIRED.*

The building isn't just moving me. It’s locking me in. I try to scream for help, but my voice is just a thin, airy rasp. I catch a glimpse of myself in the dusty window. I can barely see my face. I look like a smudge on a dirty lens.

I sink to my knees. The floor feels freezing, but I can't tell if it's the floor or my own skin. The hum reaches a peak. It's a physical weight on my shoulders, pinning me down.

I look for the metallic component I gave Chloe. It’s gone. The space where it sat is just a shimmering ripple in the air. 

The phone screen starts to melt into the plastic frame. A progress bar appears at the bottom. It’s at 99%.

A voice comes through the speaker. It’s not a person. It’s a thousand voices layered on top of each other, a wet, whispering mess.

"Data integrity compromised," the voices hiss. "Re-initializing loop."

The lights in the room turn a deep, bruised purple. The wall behind me starts to crack. A thick, black liquid seeps from the fissures. It doesn't drip. It crawls. It moves like a living thing toward my boots.

I try to stand, but my legs feel like they're made of smoke. I look at the phone one last time.

The bar hits 100%.

The screen goes pitch black. A single line of white text appears:

*RE-STARTING DELIVERY: APARTMENT 444.*

Behind me, the black liquid reaches my boots and begins to rise.
