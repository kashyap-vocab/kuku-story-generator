Episode 12: The Last Mile

The app says 3:15 AM.

The black liquid is thick. It smells like old oil and rot. It pulses against my boots in time with the hum. I try to crawl away, but my hands just slide on the tile. The liquid moves faster where my skin is clearest. It’s hunting for my transparency. It wants to coat the parts of me that aren't solid anymore. 

Suddenly, the liquid hits my knees. It freezes. It turns into a hard, jagged crust. The hum shifts. It stops being a weight and becomes a sharp vibration in my teeth. It makes my jaw ache. The black stuff starts to recede. It pulls back into the cracks of the floorboards like it’s being sucked into a drain. It’s like the building rejected me.

The purple light dies. A sickly yellow glow flickers on from a ceiling fixture. The drywall wall behind me cracks again. It doesn't just leak. It groans. It sounds like a heavy door being forced open by something huge. I scramble toward the crack. I reach out and pull a chunk of the drywall away. 

It’s not more wall behind it. It’s a narrow, dark gap. 

I squeeze through. My shoulder scrapes against the jagged edge. The pain is sharp, but I don't care. I land in a crawlspace. The air here is hot. It tastes like dust and old electricity. I see something glinting in the dirt. I reach out and pull out a bundle of copper wires. They run along the baseboards of the crawlspace. They disappear into the wall like veins.

I follow them deeper. They lead me into a small, cramped utility closet. It’s packed with old pipes and humming machinery. In the center of the room sits a metal box. It has several wires coming out of it. It’s a signal booster. 

It vibrates so hard that the dust on the floor dances in patterns. I can see the shapes in the dust. They look like tiny, frantic people. I reach out to touch the box, but my hand trembles. My left hand won't stay still.

The moment my fingers get close, the hum changes. It goes from a low drone to a piercing, high-frequency whine. It’s like a needle being pushed into my ear canal. My vision starts to strobe. 

One second, the room is pitch black. The next, it’s blinding white. I see flashes. A pile of old clothes. A flickering lightbulb. A face I don't recognize. I feel a sharp pain behind my eyes. My head feels like it’s going to split open. 

I try to pull my hand back, but my muscles lock up. I'm stuck. My fingers are inches from the metal casing. The strobe effect intensifies until the room seems to split in half. 

Then, I hear it. 

It’s not the AI. It’s not the many voices. It’s a real, human voice. It’s muffled, coming from inside the booster.

"Elias..." it whispers.
