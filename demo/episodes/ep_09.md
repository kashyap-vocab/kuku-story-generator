Episode 9: The Last Mile

The app says 3:10 AM.

The hallway is stretching. I can see the floorboards beneath my feet, but they look like they’re miles away. My legs feel light. Too light. I look at my hands. I can see the grey carpet through my palms. My skin is turning into a thin veil.

The hum in the walls is a physical weight. It makes my teeth ache. It’s a deep, rhythmic thrum. *Thump-thump. Thump-thump.* It feels like the building has a pulse.

I stumble forward. The hallway feels like a rubber band. It pulls me toward the end. I see a shadow. It’s big. It’s a man in a dark uniform. He stands in front of a heavy door. He blocks my path.

I try to move around him. He turns. He’s tall. Broad shoulders. A badge glints on his chest.

"Stop right there," he says. His voice is deep. It booms off the peeling wallpaper. "You're not supposed to be in this sector. Turn back."

I try to answer. My throat feels like it’s full of sand. I can’t find the words. I can’t remember how to speak. I just hold up the package. My arm feels like it belongs to someone else.

The man steps forward. He reaches for the box.

The hum spikes.

It goes from a thrum to a piercing whine. It’s a needle in my ear. The air feels thin. It’s like the building is sucking the oxygen out of my lungs. 

The man’s eyes widen. He gasps. "What... what is that noise?"

He clutches his chest. His face turns a bruised purple. His body goes rigid. He hits the floor. He falls like a tree being chopped down. *Thud.*

He doesn't move. He doesn't breathe.

The silence that follows is heavy. The walls pulse with a deep, vibrating hum. My vision strobes. I see flashes of white and grey. I see the man's badge on the floor.

I feel a wave of nausea. My stomach flips. I look at my phone. The screen is flickering with static. A new notification pops up. It’s a bright, clean font.

*Proceed to Apartment 444. Ignore the obstruction.*

I look at the door. It’s just a few feet away. I feel the pull in my chest again. It’s like a hook is caught in my ribs. It’s dragging me forward.

I move past the body. My boots feel like they’re floating. I reach the door. I look at my hands again. They are almost entirely see-through now. I can see the pulse of the building's lights through my own veins.

The app pings.

*Proceed to Apartment 444. Ignore the obstruction.*
