Episode 15: The Last Mile

The app says 3:48 AM.

I gasp. The air hits my lungs like ice water. I’m on the floor of the basement. My back is against a server rack. It’s hot. The heat radiates off the metal and into my spine. The hum isn't a scream anymore. It’s a low thrum. It vibrates in my teeth.

I look at my hands. They aren't see-through anymore. They’re solid. My palm is still pale. The skin is thin, like old parchment. I can see the faint blue of my veins. I’m out of the data. I’m back in the room.

I see a leather-bound book on a metal crate. It sits near the vats of black liquid. I crawl toward it. My knees scrape the concrete. I open it. The pages are damp. 

Forty names. 

I know these names. I saw them on the news three years ago. The residents who 'moved out' during the gas leak. I read the status next to each one.

*Status: Harvested.*

I flip to the last page. My heart stops. My name is at the bottom. *Elias Thorne.* It’s written in neat, black ink. Next to it is a date. 

It’s tomorrow’s date.

I try to stand. My legs shake like jelly. I look around the room. The visual artifacts are still here. They’re shadows in the corners now. They look like gray smoke.

I see a row of plastic bins. I open one. It’s full of wires. Tiny chips. Small sensors. These are the packages. These are the 'trigger' packages I've been carrying. I look back at the book. The ink next to my name shimmers. It looks wet. 

I'm not a delivery boy. I'm a crop.

A soft clicking sound comes from the server rack. A small screen on the side flickers to life. It’s a live feed. It shows me standing over the book. The angle is from the ceiling.

A text box appears at the bottom of the screen.

*SUBJECT 41: INITIALIZATION COMPLETE.*

A sharp pain pierces my temple. It’s the same spot where I hit my head on the bike. I try to think of my mother. I want to hear her name. 

Nothing. 

I try to remember the name of my street. 

There’s just a grey void in my head. A piece of my life is just... gone.

The screen flickers again. The text changes.

*COMMENCE FINAL EXTRACTION.*

The hum jumps. It becomes a deafening, bone-shaking pitch. The black liquid in the vats begins to pour upward. It defies gravity. It flows toward the ceiling in long, oily ribbons.
